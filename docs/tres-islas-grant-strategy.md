## 3. CASE STATEMENT

> Use this as the narrative backbone of every grant application. Adapt as needed for each funder.

### THE NEED

Mexico faces a devastating crisis in child welfare. An estimated **1.8 million children live in over 700 orphanages** across the country, many placed there not because they have no parents, but because their families cannot afford to feed them. Despite the enormity of this crisis, the **Mexican government allocates less than 2% of its national budget to child protection**, leaving hundreds of orphanages chronically underfunded and children vulnerable to hunger, neglect, and exploitation.

In **Mazatlan, Sinaloa** -- a tourist destination that masks deep poverty -- six orphanage homes care for over 160 of the most vulnerable children in the region: abandoned newborns, neglected toddlers, adolescent boys trying to break the cycle of poverty, and teenage girls recovering from violence and abuse. These homes receive minimal or inconsistent government support. Some receive small contributions from the Catholic Church or the Salvation Army. Some receive nothing at all.

**Without external support, these children would go hungry.**

### OUR RESPONSE

For nearly **40 years**, the Tres Islas Orphanage Fund has been the largest and most consistent external supporter of these six homes. We are not an orphanage operator -- we are a **lifeline**.

Every two weeks, our on-the-ground facilitator in Mazatlan purchases fresh food from local markets and delivers it directly to each home. We don't send cash. We don't ship containers. We buy groceries, we retain receipts, and we watch the food go into the pantry. This system has operated without a single accounting discrepancy in nearly four decades.

Beyond food, we provide:
- **Hygiene supplies** (soap, toilet paper, personal care products)
- **School supplies, uniforms, backpacks, and tutoring**
- **Medical and dental care**
- **Facility repairs** (roofs, kitchens, bathrooms, refrigerators, washers)
- **Psychological support** (especially for FloreSer's survivors of abuse)
- **Holiday celebrations** (Christmas, birthdays, special outings)

### WHAT MAKES US DIFFERENT

1. **100% of donations go directly to the children.** All board members serve without compensation. There are no paid staff. No administrative overhead is deducted from donations.

2. **40 years of unbroken service.** We are not a startup. We are not experimenting. We have fed these children through recessions, pandemics, political crises, and natural disasters.

3. **Total financial transparency.** Every purchase is made locally, every receipt is retained, every dollar is accounted for. Donors are invited to visit the homes and see the impact firsthand.

4. **Deep community trust.** Our facilitator, Gaudelia Cruz, has been on the ground since the beginning. The orphanage directors trust us. The children know us. We are family.

5. **Leverage.** A small amount of money goes extraordinarily far in Mazatlan. **$50/month feeds one child. $100/month covers food, clothing, education, and personal care.** A $10,000 grant feeds 16 children for an entire year.

### THE ASK

We are seeking grant support to:
- **Sustain our bi-weekly food delivery program** ($60,000/year to feed 160+ children)
- **Fund educational materials and tutoring** ($12,000/year)
- **Provide mental health services for FloreSer residents** ($8,000/year)
- **Cover facility maintenance and emergency repairs** ($15,000/year)

**Total annual program cost: approximately $95,000-$165,000**

- Infants and toddlers abandoned at birth

## APPENDIX: KEY TALKING POINTS FOR FUNDER CONVERSATIONS

Use these when speaking to foundation program officers, Rotary clubs, or potential corporate sponsors:

1. **"We've been doing this for 40 years."** Longevity is rare and powerful. Most funders are tired of funding organizations that disappear after 3 years.

2. **"One hundred percent of your money goes to the children."** This is your strongest line. Use it every time.

3. **"$50 feeds a child for a month."** Make the math tangible. A $5,000 grant feeds 8 children for an entire year.

4. **"We have receipts for every purchase we've ever made."** Financial transparency is what funders worry about most with international organizations. You've already solved this problem.

5. **"We don't send cash. We buy food and deliver it."** This eliminates the #1 concern funders have about international giving.

6. **"160 children depend on us. We are their largest supporter."** This isn't abstract. These are real children in real homes who eat because of this organization.

7. **"We're not a startup. We're a 40-year institution that has never had a paid employee."** This is extraordinary. Lean into it.

8. **"FloreSer is a home for teenage girls who survived violence and abuse."** This is your most emotionally compelling program. Lead with it when speaking to funders who care about gender, violence prevention, or mental health.
